# Qwen3.8 Leads a Rebuilt 128 GB Field

**The strongest open-weight model that runs in 128 GB as of late November 2026 is Alibaba's Qwen3.8-Flash-Next**, a 125B mixture-of-experts with 6B active parameters. It scores **28 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5**, the best in its class and four points behind the 552B DeepSeek V4.1 Flash and the 753B GLM-5.3. Only the 2.8T-parameter Kimi K3.1 Pro (46–47) is far ahead. There are two catches: it fits only as a ~94 GB 4-bit build, and its Qwen Community License requires model-as-a-service, AI-assistant and AI-coding businesses to get a separate licence for commercial use. **The safest high-precision pick is the Apache-2.0 Qwen3.8-27B**, which fits in full BF16 at 55.6 GB and scores 20. Next come MIT-licensed Ling-3.2-air (23 per its card), Nanbeige4.3-33B (20), the 20 GB Inkling 2 Mini (20.1) and NVIDIA's Nemotron 3.5 Super VL. Nemotron's official 75 GB NVFP4 build carries a permissive licence and a 1M-token context. Every primary pick on the eighteen-model shortlist shipped in 2026, and the models already being archived sit well back on the same scale: gpt-oss-120b scores 4 and DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 scores 23. The recommended eleven-repo download set totals **612.5 GB measured on Hugging Face**, inside the ~650 GB budget, and covers reasoning, agentic work, coding, vision, OCR, small/fast and fully open models. Treat close rankings as provisional: almost every task benchmark here is vendor-reported, harness choice alone moves agentic scores by 10–20 points, and the neutral SWE-bench, Aider and BFCL boards have not caught up with the 2026 releases.

## A 6B-active model now sits four points behind 550B rivals

Artificial Analysis rebuilt its Intelligence Index on 2026-10-27 around nine harder evaluations spread across knowledge work, coding, science and general knowledge, including FrontierCode 2.0, Terminal-Bench 4.0 and HLE-Diamond. About 60% of it now runs on private test sets, so **v5 scores cannot be compared with earlier versions** ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/artificial-analysis-intelligence-index-v5)). On that scale the open-weights leader is **Kimi K3.1 Pro at 46–47**, a 2.8T-parameter model whose 1.56 TB checkpoint went public on 2026-11-02. DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 and GLM-5.3 follow at 32 ([AA open-source models](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/open-source); [HF moonshotai listing](https://huggingface.co/api/models?author=moonshotai&sort=createdAt&direction=-1)). Inside the 128 GB envelope, **Qwen3.8-Flash-Next scores 28** and Ling-3.2-air 23 by its own card, while three ~27–33B models cluster at 20: Qwen3.8-27B, Nanbeige4.3-33B and Inkling 2 Mini ([AA leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models); [Ling-3.2-air card](https://huggingface.co/inclusionAI/Ling-3.2-air); [Thinking Machines](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/)). Nemotron 3.5 Super VL scores 14, and Gemma 4 31B, Mistral Small 4, Mistral Medium 3.5 and gpt-oss-120b all land at 4–5 ([AA open-source models](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/open-source); [AA leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models)). Those single-digit scores reflect v5's punishing terminal and agentic suites more than weak fundamentals; Gemma 4 31B still posts 84.3 on GPQA Diamond and 89.2 on AIME 2026 ([Gemma 4 card](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-4-31B-it)).

Task-level evidence points the same way but is thinner and noisier. On Artificial Analysis' own Terminal-Bench 2.1 run, **Qwen3.8-Flash-Next is the top open model at 86.1%**, ahead of Kimi K3 (85.0%) and GLM-5.3-Flash (84.3%) ([benchleader](https://www.benchleader.com/benchmarks/aa_terminalbench_21)). The official tbench.ai board for Terminal-Bench 4.1 shows GLM-5.3-Flash at 37.0% and Nemotron 3.5 Super VL at 0.3%. Many open entries there ran through mini-swe-agent with reasoning switched off, so those placements measure the harness as much as the model ([tbench.ai](https://www.tbench.ai/leaderboard)). The neutral boards that used to settle such questions have stalled. swebench.com has no Verified entry after 2026-02-26, the newest entry on Aider's polyglot board is dated 2025-10-03, and Berkeley's BFCL V4 lists no open model newer than GLM-4.6 ([SWE-bench](https://www.swebench.com/); [Aider](https://aider.chat/docs/leaderboards/); [BFCL](https://gorilla.cs.berkeley.edu/leaderboard.html)). That leaves vendor cards, and their harnesses diverge sharply. Qwen3.6-35B-A3B scores 73.4 on SWE-bench Verified in Qwen's scaffold, 70.12 in NVIDIA's NeMo Gym and 64.40 in Kwaipilot's Claude Code harness ([Qwen3.6 card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B); [Nemotron 3.5 Lightning card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Lightning-30B-A3B-BF16); [KAT-Coder card](https://huggingface.co/Kwaipilot/KAT-Coder-V2.5-Dev)).

Human-preference and usage data barely reach this size class. On arena.ai's text board, updated 2026-11-22, no open model makes the top 15, and the best in-class entry is Gemma 4 31B at #84. On the vision board, Gemma 4 31B scores 1,265 against 1,309 for the leader ([arena text](https://arena.ai/leaderboard/text); [arena vision](https://arena.ai/leaderboard/vision)). OpenRouter's weekly token ranking is dominated by open MoEs too large for this budget: DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017, Tencent's Hy4 flash and GLM-5.3-Flash ([OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/rankings)). The same boards show how dated the existing archive is. GLM-4.6, Qwen3-235B-A22B-Instruct-2507 and DeepSeek-V3.2 rank #131–#136 on arena text ([arena text](https://arena.ai/leaderboard/text)).

The turnover since that archive was chosen is nearly total, and most of its models now have an in-class successor. Qwen3.8-27B replaced Qwen3.6-27B and lifted OSWorld-Verified from 63.9 to 84.3 ([Qwen3.8-27B card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B)). Mistral Small 4 folds the Magistral, Pixtral and Devstral roles into one model, and Mistral says Medium 3.5 supersedes Devstral outright ([Mistral Small 4 announcement](https://mistral.ai/news/mistral-small-4/); [Mistral Medium 3.5 card](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Mistral-Medium-3.5-128B)). NVIDIA's current Super model is Nemotron 3.5 Super VL, released in October after March's Nemotron 3 Super ([Nemotron 3.5 Super VL card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Super-VL-120B-A12B-NVFP4)). ServiceNow's Apriel-1.6-15b-Thinker follows the archived 1.5 ([Apriel 1.6 card](https://huggingface.co/ServiceNow-AI/Apriel-1.6-15b-Thinker)). Meta moved its open line to Muse Glimmer in a new `meta-models` org, while `meta-llama` has published nothing since Llama 4 ([HF meta-models listing](https://huggingface.co/api/models?author=meta-models&sort=createdAt&direction=-1&limit=30)). DeepSeek's in-class option is V4-Flash-0731, which needs a 3-bit quant, and GLM's is GLM-5.3-Flash, which needs 2-bit; Kimi's successors are all trillion-scale ([unsloth DeepSeek GGUF](https://huggingface.co/api/models/unsloth/DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731-GGUF?blobs=true); [unsloth GLM GGUF](https://huggingface.co/api/models/unsloth/GLM-5.3-Flash-GGUF?blobs=true); [HF moonshotai listing](https://huggingface.co/api/models?author=moonshotai&sort=createdAt&direction=-1&limit=25)). The "Flash" label no longer means small. GLM-5.3-Flash is 320B, MiniMax-M3.1-Flash about 428B, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 552B, Hy4 flash 220B and ERNIE 5.5 Flash Preview 280B ([GLM-5.3-Flash card](https://huggingface.co/zai-org/GLM-5.3-Flash); [MiniMax-M3.1-Flash card](https://huggingface.co/MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-M3.1-Flash); [AA open-source models](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/open-source); [Hy4-flash card](https://huggingface.co/tencent/Hy4-flash); [ERNIE 5.5 card](https://huggingface.co/baidu/ERNIE-5.5-Flash-Preview)). The real 128 GB tier is now ~120B MoEs with 5–12B active parameters, plus dense 27–33B models that fit at full precision.

### Reasoning and agentic: Flash-Next leads, Qwen3.8-27B anchors

**Qwen3.8-Flash-Next is the capability pick, and the riskiest.** Qwen calls it "an experimental preview of the architecture that will underpin Qwen4": a 125B MoE with 6B active parameters, plus a 51B n-gram embedding table and 4B of multi-token-prediction heads, built on Gated DeltaNet and a new Qwen Sparse Attention. Its card reports SWE-bench Pro 62.5, DeepSWE 58.7, Toolathlon-Verified 73.5, LiveCodeBench v6 91.9, GPQA Diamond 91.7 and HLE 35.9, ahead of DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 on every row of the same table ([Qwen3.8-Flash-Next card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next)). Three cautions apply. First, the **Qwen Community License 1.0** requires any licensee running a model-as-a-service or "AI Work Assistant" business, a category it defines to include AI-assisted coding products, to obtain a separate licence for commercial use; internal use is exempt ([LICENSE](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next/blob/main/LICENSE)). Second, the tooling is young. The FP8 checkpoint (185.5 GB) does not fit, and the architecture is not yet in vLLM's main support table. NVIDIA's FP4-Local build, produced with quantization-aware distillation (102.26 GB; GPQA 91.5 against 92.2 for BF16), needs community vLLM containers ([NVIDIA FP4-Local card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-FP4-Local); [vLLM supported models](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/main/docs/models/supported_models.md)). Third, one single-Spark tester found it accepted `tool_choice=required` but returned no tool calls, and recommended Qwen3.8-27B for agent loops instead ([classmethod](https://dev.classmethod.jp/en/articles/dgx-spark-single-node-flash-next-vs-glm-3bit-vs-27b/)). For coding with more memory to spare, ISTA-DASLab's 50%-expert-pruned, code-calibrated GGUF keeps 79.2 of BF16's 82.8 on SWE-bench Verified in 58.4 GB ([ISTA-DASLab card](https://huggingface.co/ISTA-DASLab/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-GSQ-RCO-Coder-GGUF)).

**Qwen3.8-27B is the anchor.** It is Apache-2.0, dense and natively multimodal, and at 55.6 GB in BF16 it leaves roughly 70 GB of a 128 GB box free for context. Its card reports Terminal-Bench 2.1 73.0, SWE-bench Pro 61.7, LiveCodeBench v6 90.3, GPQA Diamond 89.2 and OSWorld-Verified 84.3, with an adjustable reasoning effort ([Qwen3.8-27B card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B)). Being dense costs speed: about 12 tok/s on a single DGX Spark at NVFP4, rising to 21 tok/s with the model's built-in MTP heads ([classmethod](https://dev.classmethod.jp/en/articles/dgx-spark-single-node-flash-next-vs-glm-3bit-vs-27b/)).

**Ling-3.2-air** (MIT) is the fastest high scorer. It has 124B total but only 5.5B active parameters, on hybrid linear attention (Kimi Delta Attention plus gated MLA) that keeps KV growth low. Its card cites 23 on AA v5.0.1 and 27 on Terminal-Bench 4.0 ([Ling-3.2-air card](https://huggingface.co/inclusionAI/Ling-3.2-air)). It was released on 2026-11-23, so no independent evaluation exists yet. The official FP8 (129.3 GB) just misses, which makes bartowski's Q4_K_M (80.4 GB) or Q5_K_M (97.6 GB) the practical builds ([HF API](https://huggingface.co/api/models/bartowski/Ling-3.2-air-GGUF?blobs=true)).

**Nanbeige4.3-33B** is an Apache-2.0, dense, Llama-architecture text model with a 512K context ([HF API](https://huggingface.co/api/models/Nanbeige/Nanbeige4.3-33B)). It tops AA's small-model Progress Index at 312 and claims Terminal-Bench 2.1 80.9 against Qwen3.8-27B's 73.0 in the same harness ([AA small models](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/open-source/small); [Nanbeige card](https://huggingface.co/Nanbeige/Nanbeige4.3-33B)).

**Inkling 2 Mini** ties Qwen3.8-27B at 20.1 on Thinking Machines' reproduction of AA v5 with about an eighth of the active compute. It has 26.4B total and 3.4B active parameters, looped three times, and ships natively as a 20 GB NVFP4/MXFP8 checkpoint with 1M context. Its card admits weaker output outside English ([Inkling 2 Mini card](https://huggingface.co/thinkingmachines/Inkling-2-Mini)).

**Nemotron 3.5 Super VL** scores lower on AA (14) but is the most deployable large model. It is OpenMDW-1.1, and its official 75 GB NVFP4 build stays within about a point of BF16 on most rows. It adds 1M context, vision and a DGX Spark serving recipe, with SWE-bench Verified 71.07, Terminal-Bench 2.1 62.78 and BrowseComp 77.5 ([Nemotron 3.5 Super VL card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Super-VL-120B-A12B-NVFP4)).

**Muse Glimmer 30B** and **Sarvam-105B-1711** round out the tier. Meta's dense model leads on MCP Atlas (75.5) and ships a DFlash drafter measured at 3.1× on an RTX 5090 ([Muse Glimmer card](https://huggingface.co/meta-models/Muse-Glimmer-30B)). Sarvam's 10.3B-active MoE posts IFEval 96.5 and τ²-Bench 85.5 and is strong in Indian languages ([Sarvam card](https://huggingface.co/sarvamai/sarvam-105b-1711)).

### Coding: generalists now outscore the specialists

The coding specialists have been overtaken. On vendor-reported SWE-bench Pro, Qwen3.8-27B (61.7) and Flash-Next (62.5) far outscore Qwen3-Coder-Next (42.7) and KAT-Coder-V2.5-Dev (45.96). The harnesses differ, and Qwen scores a corrected Pro task set ([Qwen3.8-27B card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B); [Qwen3-Coder-Next report](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00729v1); [KAT-Coder card](https://huggingface.co/Kwaipilot/KAT-Coder-V2.5-Dev)). **Qwen3-Coder-Next still earns its place for speed and provenance**: it is Apache-2.0 with 3B active parameters and 262K context. Its technical report gives SWE-bench Verified 70.6–71.3 across three scaffolds and Aider Polyglot 66.2, and it decodes at about 43 tok/s on a single DGX Spark in FP8 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00729v1); [NVIDIA forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/how-to-run-qwen3-coder-next-on-spark/359571)). **KAT-Coder-V2.5-Dev** (Apache-2.0, 35B/3B, BF16 only at 69.3 GB) scored 69.40 on Verified in a Claude Code harness that Kwaipilot says runs about 10 points below Qwen's official numbers ([KAT-Coder card](https://huggingface.co/Kwaipilot/KAT-Coder-V2.5-Dev)). **Devstral Small 2** (Apache-2.0, 25.8 GB in FP8) is the only sub-30 GB coder with an independent score: 56.4% on swebench.com's mini-SWE-agent board, ahead of its 123B sibling Devstral 2 at 53.8% ([SWE-bench](https://www.swebench.com/)). **Step-3.7-Flash** (Apache-2.0, 198B/11B, vision) fits through StepFun's own Q3_K_M GGUF and claims SWE-bench Pro 56.3 and Terminal-Bench 2.1 59.5 ([Step-3.7-Flash card](https://huggingface.co/stepfun-ai/Step-3.7-Flash)).

Mistral Medium 3.5 posts the highest SWE-bench Verified score within reach, 77.6%. It is a dense 128B model, though, decoding at about 2 tok/s on a Spark (7–9 with EAGLE), and its modified MIT licence grants no rights to companies with more than $20M in monthly revenue ([Mistral Medium 3.5 card](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Mistral-Medium-3.5-128B); [NVIDIA forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/performance-report-mistral-medium-3-5-128b-nvfp4-eagle/368917); [LICENSE](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Mistral-Medium-3.5-128B/blob/main/LICENSE)). Licence traps cluster in coding. MiniMax-M2.7 is non-commercial even though unsloth's GGUF metadata labels it "modified-mit", and Codestral (MNPL) and Salesforce's xLAM-2 (CC-BY-NC-4.0) are non-commercial too ([MiniMax LICENSE](https://huggingface.co/MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-M2.7/raw/main/LICENSE); [HF API](https://huggingface.co/api/models/unsloth/MiniMax-M2.7-GGUF?blobs=true); [Codestral](https://huggingface.co/api/models/mistralai/Codestral-22B-v0.1?blobs=true); [xLAM-2](https://huggingface.co/api/models/Salesforce/xLAM-2-32b-fc-r?blobs=true)).

### Vision, documents and audio: Qwen leads agents, Inkling leads documents

Every current Qwen release is natively multimodal, and Qwen says its Qwen3.5 early-fusion models already beat the dedicated Qwen3-VL line ([Qwen3.5 card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-122B-A10B)). For agentic vision, **Qwen3.8-27B** leads with OSWorld-Verified 84.3, AndroidWorld 81.9 and MathVision 90.0 ([Qwen3.8-27B card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B)). In its own table, **Inkling 2 Mini** leads documents and screen grounding, with OmniDocBench v1.6 92.8 and ScreenSpot-Pro 76.5 against Qwen3.8-27B's 91.5 and 57.6. Qwen keeps a slight MMMU-Pro edge, 76.3 to 74.9 ([Inkling 2 Mini card](https://huggingface.co/thinkingmachines/Inkling-2-Mini)). **Qwen3.6-35B-A3B** has the most complete classic suite and the best video numbers: MMMU 81.7, MathVista 86.4 and VideoMME 86.6 ([Qwen3.6 card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B)). **Gemma 4 31B** trails Qwen on most vision rows. It does, however, post the only in-class LMArena vision score that could be read (1,265), and it has the broadest runtime support, including a listing in llama.cpp's official multimodal docs ([arena vision](https://arena.ai/leaderboard/vision); [llama.cpp multimodal](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/docs/multimodal.md)). Cross-card comparisons need care: Gemma 4 31B's ScreenSpot-Pro is 75.9 in Meta's table and 5.9 in Thinking Machines' ([Muse Glimmer card](https://huggingface.co/meta-models/Muse-Glimmer-30B); [Inkling 2 Mini card](https://huggingface.co/thinkingmachines/Inkling-2-Mini)). Quantization also hurts agentic vision more than perception. Nemotron 3.5 Super VL's NVFP4 build loses 11 points on WebVoyager (77.72 → 66.31) while matching BF16 on MathVision ([Nemotron card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Super-VL-120B-A12B-NVFP4)).

The specialists are small and cheap to keep. **PaddleOCR-VL-1.6** (0.96B, Apache-2.0) leads OmniDocBench v1.6 at 96.33, ahead of GLM-OCR at 95.22 (MIT) ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03264)). Datalab's chandra-ocr-2 is restricted to research, personal use and startups under $2M ([chandra-ocr-2 card](https://huggingface.co/datalab-to/chandra-ocr-2)). Microsoft's Fara1.5-27B (MIT, 54.7 GB) is a screenshot-only web agent scoring 89.3 on WebVoyager ([Fara1.5 card](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/Fara1.5-27B)). For audio, NVIDIA's Nemotron-3-Nano-Omni-30B-A3B takes video, audio, image and text input (35.2 GB in FP8, under the NVIDIA Open Model Agreement). Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B adds speech output under Apache-2.0 (70.5 GB, from September 2025), and Gemma 4 12B and E4B accept audio input ([Nemotron Omni card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/Nemotron-3-Nano-Omni-30B-A3B-Reasoning-BF16); [Qwen3-Omni card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Instruct); [Gemma 4 card](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-4-31B-it)).

### Small, fast and fully open: active parameters set the speed

Speed on these boxes tracks active parameters, which is why the small tier is mostly MoE. Artificial Analysis lists output speeds of 187 tok/s for Inkling 2 Mini, 103 for Muse Glimmer, 101 for Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and 66 for dense Qwen3.8-27B ([AA small models](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/open-source/small)). Among tiny models, AA's Progress Index ranks K2 Horizon 3.7B (242), **MiniCPM5-2B** (221) and LFM3-1.7B (216) on top ([AA tiny models](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/open-source/tiny)). MiniCPM5-2B reports MMLU-Pro 70.8 and GPQA 70.2 in 5 GB under Apache-2.0 ([MiniCPM5-2B card](https://huggingface.co/openbmb/MiniCPM5-2B)). Liquid's LFM models withhold commercial rights from companies with $10M or more in annual revenue ([LFM licence](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/LFM2.5-24B-A2B/blob/main/LICENSE)). gpt-oss-20b (13.8 GB, native MXFP4, Apache-2.0) has no successor and remains a useful small reference ([gpt-oss-20b card](https://huggingface.co/openai/gpt-oss-20b)). Most labs now ship dedicated drafters for speculative decoding; Google's Gemma 4 "-assistant" models are under 1 GB and are claimed to give up to 3× speedups without quality loss ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/multi-token-prediction-gemma-4/?authuser=0)).

**K2-Horizon-32B** is the fully open pick. It comes from the Institute of Foundation Models, LLM360's successor, and ships Apache-2.0 weights with the training data, recipe, training code, intermediate checkpoints and W&B logs. By the AA figures on its card it scores GPQA Diamond 82.8 and Terminal-Bench 2.1 62.6, far ahead of Olmo 3.1 32B Think (GPQA 57.5) and Apertus v1.5 70B (35.94) ([K2-Horizon-32B card](https://huggingface.co/IFM/K2-Horizon-32B); [Olmo 3.1 card](https://huggingface.co/allenai/Olmo-3.1-32B-Think); [Apertus card](https://huggingface.co/swiss-ai/Apertus-v1.5-70B)). Licensing loosened in 2026. Google moved Gemma 4 to Apache-2.0, Meta released Muse Glimmer under Apache-2.0, and NVIDIA adopted the Linux Foundation's permissive OpenMDW-1.1 for Nemotron, while Alibaba moved the other way on its larger Qwen3.8 models ([Gemma 4 card](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-4-31B-it); [Muse Glimmer card](https://huggingface.co/meta-models/Muse-Glimmer-30B); [Linux Foundation](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/linux-foundation-releases-openmdw-1.1-nvidia-adopts-openmdw-for-cosmos-isaac-gr00t-ising-and-nemotron-ai-model-families)). For grounded RAG rather than reasoning, AI21's Jamba2 Mini (Apache-2.0, 55.1 GB in FP8, 262K context) is built to deliver "precise question answering without the computational overhead of reasoning models" ([Jamba2 Mini card](https://huggingface.co/ai21labs/AI21-Jamba2-Mini)).

## Plan for 95–105 GB of weights, not 128

Nominal capacity overstates usable memory. One tester saw a DGX Spark expose 121 GiB, and a 128 GB Mac gives the GPU only about 96 GB by default ([classmethod](https://dev.classmethod.jp/en/articles/dgx-spark-single-node-flash-next-vs-glm-3bit-vs-27b/); [llmconfigurator](https://llmconfigurator.com/en/reports/best-local-coding-models-by-hardware)). On a 128 GB Strix Halo, a 92.9 GB build of Qwen3.8-Flash-Next ran at 131K context while unsloth's 111 GB UD-Q4_K_XL crashed at 32K ([sleepingrobots](https://sleepingrobots.com/dreams/qwen38-flash-next-strix-halo/)), and a Framework-forum user found Q4 "the largest Flash-Next quant that fit on Strix Halo alone" ([Framework forum](https://community.frame.work/t/qwen3-8-please-share-your-t-s-any-quant/84405)). So treat **under ~80 GB of weights as comfortable and 95–105 GB as a tight ceiling**. Near that ceiling there is little room left for KV cache or a vision projector. On a 128 GB Mac, anything above about 96 GB also exceeds the default GPU allocation, which already shuts out Flash-Next's FP4-Local build and Step-3.7-Flash's Q3_K_M set. The hybrid-attention designs that dominate the class help. On a single Spark, Qwen3.8-27B at NVFP4 left room for about 1.27M tokens of KV cache, and Flash-Next ran in 79.4 GiB resident with about 646K tokens of KV ([classmethod](https://dev.classmethod.jp/en/articles/dgx-spark-single-node-flash-next-vs-glm-3bit-vs-27b/)).

Format follows hardware. NVFP4 and ModelOpt checkpoints (Nemotron 3.5 Super VL, Mistral Small 4, Inkling 2 Mini, NVIDIA's FP4-Local) target Blackwell parts such as the DGX Spark's GB10; Nemotron's card carries a vLLM configuration "intended for DGX Spark (GB10) only" ([Nemotron card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Super-VL-120B-A12B-NVFP4)). On Strix Halo or a Mac, the practical route is GGUF plus an mmproj file under llama.cpp, or an MLX conversion. Check the projector type against your llama.cpp build: LM Studio's bundled build once rejected Gemma 4 12B's projector as an "unknown projector type" ([llama.cpp multimodal](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/docs/multimodal.md); [LM Studio issue](https://github.com/lmstudio-ai/lmstudio-bug-tracker/issues/2021)). Decode speed follows active parameters. On a single Spark, Qwen3-Coder-Next FP8 ran at about 43 tok/s and Mistral Small 4 NVFP4 at 28.8 tok/s. Flash-Next managed 17.5 tok/s (28.0 with MTP) and dense Qwen3.8-27B 12.4 (20.8 with MTP), while dense Mistral Medium 3.5 crawled at about 2 tok/s without EAGLE ([NVIDIA forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/how-to-run-qwen3-coder-next-on-spark/359571); [NVIDIA forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/running-mistral-small-4-119b-moe-on-dgx-spark-with-sglang-full-setup-benchmarks/364763); [classmethod](https://dev.classmethod.jp/en/articles/dgx-spark-single-node-flash-next-vs-glm-3bit-vs-27b/); [NVIDIA forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/performance-report-mistral-medium-3-5-128b-nvfp4-eagle/368917)). Strix Halo shows the same pattern at lower speeds. Flash-Next at UD-IQ4_XS decodes at about 16–21 tok/s at 16–32K context, and a dense 70B at Q4 manages 5.1 tok/s ([Framework forum](https://community.frame.work/t/qwen3-8-please-share-your-t-s-any-quant/84405); [visorcraft](https://github.com/visorcraft/strix-halo-llm-perf)).

At 4 bits quantization costs little; below that it costs real quality. RedHat's NVFP4 build of the 501B Beam 1.0 recovers about 99% of BF16 across 22 benchmarks, and unsloth describes its dynamic 4- and 5-bit GGUFs as "mostly lossless" ([RedHatAI](https://huggingface.co/RedHatAI/Beam-1.0-501B-A23B-NVFP4); [unsloth GLM-5.2 guide](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/glm-5.2)). Agentic tasks are the exception: Nemotron 3.5 Super VL's NVFP4 build drops DeepSWE from 24.65 to 18.2 and WebVoyager by 11 points ([Nemotron card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Super-VL-120B-A12B-NVFP4)). At 2 bits, unsloth measures about 82% top-1 token agreement with the full model. It warns that 1-bit quants loop, return empty answers and "should not be used for tool calling or agentic work", and names UD-Q2_K_XL as the lowest bit width for real use ([unsloth GLM-5.2 guide](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/glm-5.2); [unsloth Dynamic 3.0](https://unsloth.ai/docs/basics/dynamic-3.0-ggufs)). The practical rule: **prefer a model that fits at 3–5 bits over a bigger one squeezed to 2**.

Flash-Next adds a twist that will matter more as embedding-heavy designs spread: its file size overstates its memory needs. Its n-gram table holds 51.2B values, stored in unsloth's UD-IQ4_XS set as a separate 28.8 GB IQ4_NL shard. llama.cpp b12233 and later can load a table of a different precision with `--ple`, and the same repo offers BF16, Q8_0 and Q5_1 versions ([unsloth engram README](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-GGUF/blob/main/engram/README.md)). Qwen calls n-gram embeddings "more amenable to offloading than Mixture-of-Experts". The Strix Halo report shows the table paging from SSD via mmap, and NVIDIA's FP4-Local card promises 1M tokens of FP8 KV cache on a Spark "with PLE offload" ([Qwen3.8-Flash-Next card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next); [sleepingrobots](https://sleepingrobots.com/dreams/qwen38-flash-next-strix-halo/); [NVIDIA FP4-Local card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-FP4-Local)).

## Conclusion

The way to fill 128 GB has flipped. The old route was squeezing the biggest MoE available into 2–3 bits: a Qwen3-235B-class model, the size of the one already in the archive, needs a 104.7 GiB Q3_K_M to run on a Strix Halo ([visorcraft](https://github.com/visorcraft/strix-halo-llm-perf)). That route now loses to the newest ~120B MoEs with 5–12B active parameters at 4 bits, and to dense 27–33B models at full precision. GLM-5.3-Flash shows why: it matches Flash-Next's 28 at full precision but fits only at 2 bits, where unsloth measured about 18% top-1 token disagreement on its predecessor. Licence and capability also now pull in opposite directions at the top of the class. The best-scoring model carries the most restrictive terms, so the archive should hold a permissive fallback at every tier. This set does: Qwen3.8-27B (Apache), Ling-3.2-air (MIT) and Nemotron 3.5 Super VL (OpenMDW) sit right behind Flash-Next.

Two things will move this list soon. Four shortlisted models (Ling-3.2-air, Inkling 2 Mini, Sarvam-105B-1711 and Nanbeige4.3-33B) are under five weeks old and backed mostly by vendor evidence, and ERNIE 5.5 Flash Preview could join once a reputable ~3-bit quant appears. Re-rank when Artificial Analysis and tbench.ai results land. And as runtimes learn to page embedding tables from SSD, as llama.cpp's `--ple` already does for Flash-Next, "fits in 128 GB" will become a question about resident weights rather than file size. That could, though it is untested, bring embedding-heavy models such as Hy4 flash, whose 220B includes 64B of Engram tables, within reach.
