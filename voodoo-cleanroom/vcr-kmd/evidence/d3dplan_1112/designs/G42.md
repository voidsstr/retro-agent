## 0. Summary

**The gap is real, and it has a fourth part that hal-29 did not list.**

1. **Capacity.** One 4096-slot table serves every process (`vcrdd_d3d.c:45, 84`). `name_surface` (`:1921-1949`) enters every surface the runtime gives a handle:
   - textures and **every mip level**;
   - flip chains and Z buffers;
   - system-memory backing chains;
   - **D3D8 vertex and index buffers.**

   At 4097 entries `handle_set` refuses (`:109-111`). Every DP2 consumer of that handle then fails silently:
   - the texture draws untextured;
   - TEXBLT copies nothing, which leaves stale video memory in the texture;
   - SETRENDERTARGET keeps drawing into the previous target.
2. **Cost.** Every destroyed surface rebuilds the whole table (`handle_forget`, `:134-146`):
   - a 4096-slot scan, a 48 KB `memset`, and a re-insert of every survivor;
   - that is about 6000 slot visits at 2000 live entries **(model)**;
   - it happens once per mip level at DestroySurface, and once more per level at the destroy notice.
3. **Lookups.** With one process the old hash puts consecutive handles in consecutive slots. The table then behaves like an array: one probe per lookup, until the cliff. After the cliff, every lookup of a refused handle visits all 4096 slots (two such lookups per `compute_regs`). With two DirectDraw locals the two runs overlap, and absent-key lookups average about 500 slots **(model)**.
4. **(New.) Two cached-pointer hazards in the same lifetime code.**
   - `VcrDdD3dSurfaceGone` clears a context's `rt`/`zb`, but not `tex_surf0`/`tex_surf1`.
   - `prepare()` can run `flush_written()` on that cached pointer **before** `compute_regs` refreshes it (`:1328-1333`). That function writes `dwReserved1` (`:1297`).
   - So a bound texture destroyed between two DP2 calls is a latent use-after-free that **writes** freed memory.
   - A bound handle re-named by CreateSurfaceEx is never reported to its contexts either. 3dfx's HAL does report it (`VH5/D7D3D.C:443, 470`).

**Is the cliff reached by a staged title? Unknown, and the design measures it first.**

- The exit-time rings of 11-12 show that UE2 and Halo hold at least 1390-1948 live handles: UT2004 botmatch at least 1948, UT2003 at least 1857, Halo at least 1602. These are lower bounds.
- The rings wrap during the destroy storm, so the peak is not recorded.
- No saved ring covers gameplay. "table full" appears in no saved ring.

**The design, in two phases:**

- **Phase 1: default-on, no switch, display DLL only.**
  - **Container.** A new Win32-free container, `KMD/include/vcr_htab.h`:
    - 16384 slots, taking at most 12288 entries (3x today);
    - the slot is chosen by the **handle** alone (Fibonacci hashing, top 14 bits) and the key is (DirectDraw local, handle);
    - deletion by backward shift, with no tombstones and no rebuild;
    - a whole local is dropped in place.
  - **Destroy paths delete by key.** DestroySurface uses the surface's own handle, so no owner is needed. The destroy notice deletes `(local, handle)` only if it still names that surface.
  - **The two cached-pointer fixes.**
  - **Counters and a per-process summary line**, which carries the high-water mark asked for in hal.md Phase 0 and that G76 can export.
  - **What is named does not change.** For every key the old table held, every lookup returns what it returned before.
- **Phase 2 ("roots only"): default-OFF behind a `Diag\D3DFeat` bit.**
  - Name only what a DX7 DP2 stream can reference: not mip sub-levels (except in render-target-capable chains), not execute buffers, not non-3D surfaces.
  - This is what the DDK says (`DDKH` §4.5.8.2: *"only the top-level surface's handle is passed … in the SetTexture API call"*), and what both reference drivers do.
  - In the 11-12 exit tails it removes about 91% of UE2's entries and all of Halo's buffers.
  - It graduates only when Phase 1's reference counters show that no DP2 command named such a surface.

**Tests:**

- `tests/native/test_vcr_kmd_htab.c`: the deployed algorithm beside the new one, plus a random differential test against a reference model.
- Python source pins.
- d3dprobe `handles`: 4440 named backing surfaces, then every DP2 handle consumer past the old cliff. The deployed build must FAIL it and the new build PASS it.
- Two informational probes, `updlod` and `rtlevel`, that decide Phase 2.

**Priority P2. Effort M.**

Raise it to P1 if Boot A measures a peak above 4096 in any staged title: the deployed build was then refusing handles in play (section 10).

## 2. Evidence

### 2.1 What the 11-12 rings show (`ONBOX/games/*/ring_decoded.txt`, the exit of each session)

Event 513/3 ("CreateSurfaceEx %u") is logged for every surface the walk visits. At exit these are the runtime's **destroy notices** (`fpVidMem` 0), one per level.

| session | notices in the ring tail | handles seen | system-memory mip levels (`caps 00401800`) | execute buffers (`00810800` + `00800800`) | order |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| UT2004 botmatch (DM-Antalus) | 1008 | 4 .. **1948** | 734, in 93 runs of consecutive handles, mostly 8-10 levels | 194 + 80 | descending from 1718; 1942-1948 come 17.4 s later, at the forced kill |
| UT2004 statfps | 1011 | 4 .. 1390 | 734 | 196 + 80 | descending from 1058 |
| UT2003 flyby | 1012 | 4 .. 1857 | 819, in 100 runs (7-10 levels) | 131 + 51 | descending from 1412 |
| Halo timedemo | 1013 | 4 .. 1602 | 0 | 505 + 507 | buffers ascending 604 → 1602 |

What this shows:

- **Sub-levels and buffers dominate the table.** In the UE2 tails, about 87% of the system-memory texture entries are sub-levels (641 of 734). In Halo's tail, 1012 of 1013 entries are D3D8 vertex/index buffers (`DDSCAPS_EXECUTEBUFFER`).
- The HAL never looks either kind up (section 1).
- **Handles are reused immediately.** `retro-3dfx/FINDINGS.md:1222-1223` ("handles are reused at once, across system- and video-memory surfaces"). So the largest handle number in use approximates the peak count.
- **Cost of a destroy notice, logging included:** 1012 in 140 ms (UT2003), 1011 in 165 ms (statfps), 1013 in 273 ms (Halo). That is 0.14-0.27 ms each, and it includes one IOCTL per event (G52). The rebuild's share cannot be separated from these numbers.

### 2.2 What they cannot show

- **The peak.**
  - Each tail holds only the last ~1008 notices.
  - The order is newest-first, so the higher handles were destroyed earlier, outside the window.
  - Video-memory surfaces leave through DestroySurface, which logs nothing.
  - So the 1948 / 1857 / 1602 / 1390 above are **lower bounds**.
- **Whether the cliff was ever reached.**
  - "surface handle table full" and a NULL-surface "texture refused" appear in no saved ring under `KMD/evidence`. Searched case-insensitively.
  - But no saved ring covers gameplay, and the ring wraps within seconds (G76).
  - So this is an absence of evidence, not evidence of absence.

### 2.3 The two tables side by side (model, probe counts; the deployed algorithm re-implemented line for line)

| scenario | deployed table | proposed table |
|---|---|---|
| 1 local, 2000 handles: lookup of a present key | 1 slot | 1 slot |
| same: one destroyed surface | ~6000 slot visits (4096-slot scan + 1999 re-inserts) + 48 KB `memset` | 2 |
| 1 local, 5000 handles | **904 refused** (handles 4097-5000); a lookup of one visits 4096 slots | 0 refused; 1 slot |
| 2 locals x 1500 handles: absent-key lookup | **528 slots** on average (the runs overlap) | 1.0 |
| same: present-key lookup | 1.0 | 2.0 (the second local's entry for handle h sits beside the first's) |
| churn, 50,000 mixed ops with lowest-free reuse: worst lookup | — | 1 slot at 6000 live (1 local); 3 at 12000; 4 with 4 locals x 1500 or 2 x 6000 |
| dense handles 1..8000 in 16384 slots: worst insert | — | 1 slot (Fibonacci hashing spreads consecutive integers) |

**A trap found while modelling the bulk delete.**

- After dropping a local's entries in place, survivors are re-seated by a sweep in probe order.
- That sweep **must start at a slot that was empty before anything was cleared**.
- Starting at the first slot the clear emptied lost a key:
  - in 1 of 3000 random 256-slot trials;
  - in about 0.2% of random 16-slot trials.
- With the correct start: 0 failures in 3000 + 40,000 trials **(model)**.
- The native test pins this (section 6.1, case 6).

## 3. Design

### 3.1 Scope and phases

| part | default | changes for the runtime / hardware |
|---|---|---|
| **Phase 1**: the container (`vcr_htab.h`), key-based deletion, the two cached-pointer fixes, counters and summary | **ON, no switch** | Nothing below the old cliff: same surfaces named, every lookup answered as before. Capacity is 3x. One extra state recompute when a bound texture dies or is re-named |
| **Phase 2**: name only roots, render targets, Z and flip chains | OFF, `Diag\D3DFeat` bit `HROOTS` | Lookups of sub-level, buffer and non-3D handles return NULL. Nothing in today's HAL performs them (section 1); Phase 1's counters prove it per title |
| **Audit**: verify that no entry outlives its surface | OFF, `Diag\D3DFeat` bit `HAUDIT` (or a `-DVCR_HTAB_AUDIT` build) | none: checks and logs only, O(table) per delete |

No register, no cap, no format list and no `ddRVal` changes in any phase.

### 3.2 The container: `KMD/include/vcr_htab.h` (new, Win32-free, `vcr_types.h` only)

```c
/* vcr_htab.h - the Direct3D HAL's surface handle table (G42): (DirectDraw
 * local, surface handle) -> surface. Owner and value are opaque pointers, so
 * tests/native compiles exactly the code vcrdd_d3d.c runs.
 *
 * Open addressing with linear probing. The slot comes from the HANDLE alone
 * (Fibonacci hashing, top bits): the runtime numbers a local's surfaces 1, 2,
 * 3 ... and reuses a freed number at once, so dense handles spread over the
 * table with no collisions (1..8000 in 16384 slots: one probe), and another
 * local's handle h sits beside the first's. The local is part of the KEY, not
 * of the slot, because DdDestroySurface knows the surface and its handle but
 * not its local (an NT surface carries no owner).
 *
 * Deletion is Knuth's backward shift (TAOCP 6.4, Algorithm R): no tombstones,
 * so a lookup stops at the first empty slot and every key stays reachable.
 * A whole local is cleared and the survivors re-seated in place. */

#define VCR_HTAB_BITS      14u                       /* the driver's table: 16384 slots */
#define VCR_HTAB_HASH      0x9e3779b9u               /* 2^32 / golden ratio */

typedef struct vcr_hent { const void *owner; vcr_u32 handle; void *val; } vcr_hent;

typedef struct vcr_htab_stats {
    vcr_u32 live, peak, peak_boot, limit;      /* peak: since the table was last empty */
    vcr_u32 inserts, updates, deletes, refused;
    vcr_u32 lookups, misses, probe_max, long_lookups;   /* long: more than 8 slots */
    vcr_u32 purges, scan_fallbacks, stale_found, max_handle;
} vcr_htab_stats;

typedef struct vcr_htab {
    vcr_hent *e;                    /* 1 << bits entries, zeroed = empty */
    vcr_u32   bits, mask, limit;    /* limit = 3/4 of the slots */
    vcr_htab_stats st;
} vcr_htab;

#define VCR_HTAB_INIT(arr, b) { (arr), (b), (1u << (b)) - 1u, ((1u << (b)) / 4u) * 3u, { 0 } }
```

- The entry array is separate from the descriptor, so the driver's 192 KB array stays in `.bss` and only the small descriptor is initialised data:
  - `static vcr_hent g_hte[1u << VCR_HTAB_BITS];`
  - `static vcr_htab g_ht = VCR_HTAB_INIT(g_hte, VCR_HTAB_BITS);`
- The host test builds tiny tables (16 slots) the same way.
- `st.limit` is a copy of `limit`, for export.

Functions, all `static inline` and integer-only (the kernel build is `-mgeneral-regs-only`):

| function | contract |
|---|---|
| `vcr_htab_home(t, h)` | `(h * VCR_HTAB_HASH) >> (32 - t->bits)` |
| `vcr_htab_get(t, owner, h)` | `NULL` for `h == 0`, a NULL owner, or an absent key. Probes from home to the key or the first empty slot. Counts lookups, misses, `probe_max`, `long_lookups` |
| `vcr_htab_set(t, owner, h, v)` | 2 = updated an existing key; 1 = inserted; 0 = refused (`h == 0`, a NULL owner, or `live == limit`; `refused++`). Inserting updates `peak`, `peak_boot` and `max_handle` |
| `vcr_htab_del_at(t, i)` | backward shift. Walk `j` forward from `i` to the first empty slot. Move `e[j]` into the hole when `((j - home(e[j])) & mask) >= ((j - i) & mask)`, i.e. its home is not in `(i, j]`, and the hole moves to `j`. Empty the last hole. `live--`; `peak = 0` when `live` reaches 0 |
| `vcr_htab_del(t, owner, h, v)` | delete `(owner, h)` if present and (`v == NULL` or it names `v`); returns 1 or 0 |
| `vcr_htab_del_val(t, h, v)` | delete every entry with handle `h` naming `v`, **any owner**. It scans `h`'s run from its home to the first empty slot, and re-examines slot `i` after each `del_at`. Returns the count |
| `vcr_htab_purge(t, owner, v)` | delete every entry matching the owner (or the value), in place. **(1)** remember `e0`, the first slot that is empty **now** (one exists, because `live <= limit < size`). **(2)** clear every match. **(3)** for `d = 1 .. size-1`, re-seat the survivor at `(e0 + d) & mask`: take it out, then put it at the first empty slot from its home. That slot is never past the one just vacated. Returns the count |
| `vcr_htab_count_val(t, v)` | the audit: entries naming `v` (full scan) |

Invariants the native test checks after every operation:

- every entry is reachable from its home without crossing an empty slot;
- `live` equals the number of non-empty slots and never exceeds `limit`;
- every loop is bounded by the slot count.

| `Dd_DestroyDDLocal` `:1971-1979` | `handle_drop_owner(p->pDDLcl)` replaces `handle_forget(p->pDDLcl, NULL)` |
| `surf_kind(s)` (new) | `HK_BUF` if `DDSCAPS_EXECUTEBUFFER`. `HK_OTHER` without `TEXTURE|3DDEVICE|ZBUFFER`. `HK_SUB` if `DDSCAPS_MIPMAP` and either `lpSurfMore->ddsCapsEx.dwCaps2 & DDSCAPS2_MIPMAPSUBLEVEL` or `lpAttachListFrom->lpAttached` is a `DDSCAPS_MIPMAP` surface. The two signals are logged once if they disagree. Else `HK_TOP` |

`.bss` grows from 0x261a0 to about 0x3e1a0 (+96 KB: +192 KB of entries, −2 x 48 KB). This is nothing next to the session image space a display driver lives in **(inferred)**.

### 3.4 Why the two cached-pointer fixes belong here

Both are lifetime rules of "a surface named by handle", and both live in the two functions this item rewrites.

- **Fix 1, the bound texture that dies.** `prepare()` runs `flush_written(c, c->tex_surf0/1)` when `g_tex_writes` moved and the context is not dirty (`:1325-1333`). That is: any texture upload since this context's last triangle, with no state change before the next one. `flush_written` reads the chain and clears `SF_DIRTY` in its `dwReserved1` (`:1297`).
  - If that texture's surface was destroyed in between, this is a read **and a write** into freed session pool.
  - The window is narrow: the D3D8 texture manager evicts least-recently-used copies, and a bound texture was just used **(inferred)**. It is still a kernel memory write with no guard.
  - Pointer compares in `VcrDdD3dSurfaceGone` close it.
- **Fix 2, the re-named bound handle.** The handle number is reused at once (§2.1). A managed texture evicted and re-created under the **same** number need not be re-sent by the runtime as a TEXTUREMAP change **(inferred)**.
  - Today the first `prepare()` of the next DP2 flushes the **old** pointer, not the new texture.
  - The new texture's `SF_DIRTY` then stays set, and it can be sampled through stale TMU cache lines until a later write (G37 territory, inferred mechanism).
  - Marking the context dirty makes `compute_regs` run first, so the flush hits the surface that is actually bound.

Every line below is rare by construction: per process, per boot, or once per context. None is on the triangle path. Every `VcrDd` is an IOCTL (G52). The `what` numbers are provisional (24-29 are already contended by G01/G03/G05/G10/G11/G13-G16/G24). The plan owner assigns them; symbolic names are used here.

### 3.6 Phase 2: name only what DP2 can reference (`Diag\D3DFeat` bit `HROOTS`)

In `name_surface`, with `pd->h_roots`, where `pd` is `l->lpGbl->dhpdev`:

| walked surface | Phase 1 | Phase 2 |
|---|---|---|
| root, flip-chain member, Z, render target | named | named |
| mip sub-level of a chain whose root has **no** `DDSCAPS_3DDEVICE` | named | **skipped**, and `vcr_htab_del(&g_ht, l, h, NULL)`: the handle now names this surface, so any older name is stale (VH5's PRS 14511 rule) |
| mip sub-level of a render-target-capable chain (`DDSCAPS_3DDEVICE` on the root) | named | named: D3D8 can `SetRenderTarget` to a texture level |
| `DDSCAPS_EXECUTEBUFFER` (D3D8 VB/IB) | named | skipped, stale name deleted |
| a surface without `TEXTURE|3DDEVICE|ZBUFFER` (a windowed primary, a plain offscreen) | named | skipped, stale name deleted |
| `fpVidMem == 0` (destroy notice) | `handle_drop` | `handle_drop` (unchanged) |

- Nothing else changes. `next_mip` reaches the levels for sampling, TEXBLT and flushes.
- `handle_drop_surf` of a skipped surface finds nothing by key and does no fallback scan, because `h != 0`. So a sub-level's DestroySurface costs O(1).
- **Graduation (default-on in a later commit, with its own test):** across the staged D3D titles and every d3dprobe list, Phase 1's `HTAB_REFS` must show `sub 0 buf 0 odd 0`, and `updlod` / `rtlevel` must not contradict it.
- **It may stay off for good.** With Phase 1 the table is not short of space; Phase 2 only saves work and memory.
- Read at boot, like `D3DBigTex`, so a process never sees the policy change under it. Not gated on `napalm`: the table is chip-independent, so the 86Box Voodoo3 bed can prove it.

### 3.7 The audit (`Diag\D3DFeat` bit `HAUDIT`, or a `-DVCR_HTAB_AUDIT=1` build if the D3DFeat carrier has not landed)

- After every `handle_drop`, `handle_drop_surf` and `handle_drop_owner`, it counts the entries still naming the destroyed surface or local (`vcr_htab_count_val`, or an owner scan). Any found go to `stale_found`, a WARN `HTAB_STALE` once, and a purge.
- At `handle_set` it also counts other keys naming the same surface (a swap left half done), as `double_named`.
- Cost: one full-table scan per delete. This is for one supervised boot, never default.
- **Purpose:** prove on silicon that deleting by key leaves nothing behind. The deployed code deleted by pointer and could not leave anything behind.

### 3.8 Alternatives not taken

| option | for | against | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Per-local arrays indexed by handle, grown with `EngAllocMem` (the DDK's advice; `P2S`, `VH5/D7D3D.C:913-990`) | O(1), never full | an allocation, a copy and a failure path inside CreateSurfaceEx; sized by the **largest** handle, and the DDK warns handles are not guaranteed consecutive; a list to find per local; more code. 3dfx's version carries the DestroySurface bug above | the follow-up if a measured peak passes half of 12288 |
| B. Raise `MAX_HANDLES` only | smallest diff | keeps the O(table) rebuild per destroyed level, the merged runs with two locals, and a full scan per lookup at any cliff | rejected |
| C. Stop naming sub-levels as the only fix (hal-29's first bullet) | 87-100% fewer entries | the only part with an open correctness question (D3D8 `UpdateTexture` with a larger source, DX7 `Load` into a sub-level, `SetRenderTarget` to a texture level). Without it, Phase 1 already removes the cliff | Phase 2, gated and measured |
| D. A tag in the low bits of the stored pointer, to remember each entry's kind | no extra memory | clever, and needless: the kind is read from the surface on a hit | `surf_kind` at lookup |
| E. Fail CreateSurfaceEx with `DDERR_OUTOFMEMORY` at the limit (the DDK's general rule) | honest | turns a never-seen case into an application error (`DDKH` §4.5.7); today's tolerance is kept, but loud | open question 5 |

## 4. Gating

- **Phase 1 lands default-on, with no switch. It is safe to.**
  - It writes no register, changes no cap or format, and changes no answer the runtime sees. `CreateSurfaceEx` still answers `DD_OK`.
  - For every key the deployed table held, every lookup returns the same surface. The native differential test proves this by running the deployed algorithm beside the new one on identical sequences.
  - What changes is capacity (4096 → 12288), cost (O(table) → O(1) per destroy), logging, and two pointer clears that can only add a state recompute.
  - The proven path is not byte-for-byte unchanged in the code, but it is answer-for-answer unchanged. This is the "pure correctness fix proven by a d3dprobe test" class.
  - Its first deploy is itself the supervised Boot A (section 7.2).
  - **Rollback:** `vcrdd.dll` 95678704. `vcrmp.sys` fda1b8c9 is not touched by Phase 1.
- **Phase 2 (`HROOTS`) and the audit (`HAUDIT`) are default-OFF** `Diag\D3DFeat` bits, read at boot.
  - Provisional bit numbers: bits 10 and 11, 0x400 / 0x800. Bits 1, 2, 4, 6-9 are claimed by G08, G22, G16, G05, G12, G13, G24; the plan owner fixes the numbers.
  - Each bit is armed alone, one per clean boot.
  - `HROOTS` graduates by a later commit with its own test, under the plan-wide D3DFeat convention (a default mask or a `D3DFeatOff` kill mask, G05 §4.1).
  - `HAUDIT` never graduates.

## 5. d3dprobe tests (`KMD/tools/d3dprobe.c`, explicit `--tests` only, never in the default list)

### 5.1 `handles`: past the old cliff, then every DP2 consumer of a handle

1. **D** = `CreateTexture(32, 32, 0, 0, R5G6B5, DEFAULT)`, created early: 6 levels.
2. **S[0..739]** = `CreateTexture(32, 32, 0, 0, R5G6B5, SYSTEMMEM)`.
   - 740 x 6 = **4440 named backing surfaces** (`DDKH` §4.5.7 / §4.6.3: backing surfaces are named).
   - Level `k` of `S[i]` is filled with `col(i, k)`: r5 = (7i + 3k + 1) & 31, g6 = (13i + 11k + 5) & 63, b5 = (3i + 17k + 9) & 31. These are 565-exact, and neighbours differ by about 50 in red and green.
   - The loop is timed: `create_ms`.
3. **DL** = a second DEFAULT 32x32 chain, and **RL** = `CreateRenderTarget(64, 64, R5G6B5, NONE, 0, TRUE)`. Both are created **after** the 4440 surfaces.
4. For i in {0, 1, 370, 738, 739}: `UpdateTexture(S[i], D)` (DP2 TEXBLT by handle), then draw D with MIPFILTER POINT:
   - a 32-px quad selects level 0: expect `col(i, 0)`;
   - an 8-px quad selects level 2: expect `col(i, 2)`;
   - read at each quad's centre, tolerance 12 (the `mip` test's method).
5. `UpdateTexture(S[0], DL)`, then `SetTexture(DL)` and a 32-px quad: expect `col(0, 0)`. This exercises TEXBLT's destination and TEXTUREMAP with late handles.
6. `SetRenderTarget(RL, NULL)`, `Clear(0xff204060)`, a solid `0xffc08040` quad over the centre 32x32. Then `SetRenderTarget` back to the back buffer and Z. `LockRect(RL)`: the centre must be `c08040` and a corner `204060` (565-rounded). This exercises SETRENDERTARGET with a late handle.
7. **Churn.**
   - Release `S[even]`: 370 chains, 2220 destroy notices (`release_ms`).
   - Create `S2[0..369]` with `col(1000 + j, k)` (`recreate_ms`); the runtime reuses the freed numbers.
   - Repeat step 4 for `S2[0]`, `S2[185]`, `S2[369]`, `S[1]` and `S[739]`.
8. Release everything.
   - The RESULT carries an info entry: `{"test":"handles","named":4440,"create_ms":…,"release_ms":…,"recreate_ms":…}`.
   - The A/B of `release_ms` shows the rebuild cost: on the deployed build each of the 2220 destroy notices rebuilds a full 4096-slot table (about 8000 slot visits plus a 48 KB `memset`); on the new one each is about 2 probes **(model)**. The IOCTL per event is the same in both builds.

**Pass:** every check within tolerance, **and** the ring check (5.3): the probe's `HTAB_SUM` shows `peak >= 4440` and `refused 0`.

**On the deployed build (95678704) the expected result is FAIL:**

| check | why it fails there |
|---|---|
| S[738], S[739] | their roots were refused, so TEXBLT finds no source. D keeps the previous source's colours: about 56 off in red |
| DL | its handle was refused: TEXBLT copies nothing and the stage draws untextured (white) |
| RL | its handle was refused: SETRENDERTARGET keeps the back buffer, and RL holds unwritten video memory |
| the churn checks | can pass: the releases freed room |

The deployed build also writes one 513/9 WARN per refused surface. So A/B on one boot pair gives a crisp old-FAIL / new-PASS.

**Precondition, read from the ring:** if `peak < 4096`, the runtime did not name SYSTEMMEM backing surfaces as assumed. Report "precondition not met", not a pass.

### 5.2 `updlod` and `rtlevel`: the evidence Phase 2 needs (informational: `observe()` entries that do not count in pass/fail)

- **`updlod`.**
  - Setup: `S64` = SYSTEMMEM 64x64 with 7 levels, level colours as in `mip`; `D32` = DEFAULT 32x32 with 6 levels; then `UpdateTexture(S64, D32)`.

  | outcome | condition | meaning |
  |---|---|---|
  | `refused` | the call fails | the runtime does not allow a larger source here: no sub-level reference can come from this path |
  | `matched` | D32 drawn at 32 px and 16 px shows S64's levels 1 and 2 | read together with the ring's `sub` count at BLTS. `sub > 0`: the runtime named a source **sub-level**, so Phase 2 must keep naming SYSTEMMEM sub-levels (or resolve them through `lpAttachListFrom`, as `VH5/DDBLT32.C:688-722` does). `sub == 0`: the runtime emulated the copy another way, and naming is irrelevant |
  | `unmatched` | anything else | with `sub == 0`, the runtime sent the **root** and our TEXBLT lacks 3dfx's "skip the larger source levels" (`VH5/D6DP2.C:3892-3911`). That is a separate TEXBLT gap (open question 6), not G42 |

- **`rtlevel`.**
  - `CreateTexture(64, 64, 2, D3DUSAGE_RENDERTARGET, R5G6B5, DEFAULT)`, SKIPPED if refused.
  - `SetRenderTarget` to level 1, clear green, a red half, restore the target, then sample level 1 with a 16-px quad.
  - The ring's `sub` count at the RT site is the G42 fact: it shows whether SETRENDERTARGET named a sub-level. Phase 2's `DDSCAPS_3DDEVICE` exception exists for exactly this case.
  - The pixel result belongs to G36 (render-to-texture is unproven).

### 5.3 The harness: `d3dprobe_run.py --ring-check htab`

- After a **civil** exit (no D3D process left), run `vcrctl log` with `--tool`.
- Take the `HTAB_SUM` / `HTAB_REFS` pair whose `pid` is the probe's.
- Fail the run on `refused > 0` or `stale > 0`.
- For `handles`, require `peak >= 4440`. Below 4096, report "precondition not met", which is not a pass (like `named_skips`).
- Print `peak`, `max handle`, `pmax`, `fb` and the sub/buf/odd counts by site.
- Never runs while the probe is alive.

## 9. Effort, dependencies, titles, priority

- **Effort: M.**
  - The header is about 150 lines and the HAL wiring about 150.
  - The native test is about 350 lines, the probes about 250, the harness and pins about 80.
  - One supervised boot, plus an optional audit boot.
- **Depends on:**
  - **G01**: a clean engine for any on-box D3D check (or a cold boot with no Glide session first).
  - **G05**: the shared `Diag\D3DFeat` carrier, for Phase 2 and the audit only; G22 proposes the same carrier.
  - Softer: G75 (a DX7 probe would test `Load` into a sub-level directly), G73 (in-game runs collect Phase 2's evidence for free), G76 (may export `vcr_htab_stats`), G52 (makes the per-surface 513/3 cheap).
- **Titles:** UT2003, UT2004 and Halo have the largest measured handle counts and are the ones a cliff would hit first. Thief II NewDark and Max Payne are unmeasured.
- **Priority: P2.**
  - No staged title is known to reach the cliff, and the deletion cost is modest at today's loads.
  - Phase 1 also closes a latent kernel write-after-free and measures the unknown peak.
  - **Raise to P1** if Boot A shows a peak above 4096 for any staged title: the deployed build was then refusing handles in play.

## 10. Open questions

1. **The real peak.**
   - What is the live-handle peak of UE2 maps (DM-Antalus, an ONS map) and of Halo? The exit tails only bound it below: at least 1948 / 1857 / 1602 / 1390.
   - Above 4096 on the deployed build, refused handles drew untextured, in the vertex colour (often white), and copied nothing on TEXBLT. Then re-examine G18 (UE2 "white polygons") and G44 for refused-handle contributions.
   - G06's noise is explained by G07 (split mip chains), not by this.
2. **Sub-level and buffer references.** Does XP's runtime ever put a mip sub-level, an execute buffer or a non-3D surface handle in a DP2 command? Three candidates:
   - D3D8 `UpdateTexture` with a larger source (`updlod`);
   - DX7 `IDirect3DDevice7::Load` into a sub-level (needs G75's DX7 probe or in-game data);
   - D3D8 `SetRenderTarget` to a texture level (`rtlevel`).

   The DDK (§4.5.8.2) says the SetTexture path never does.
3. **Handles at DestroySurface.** Does the runtime keep `lpSurfMore->dwSurfaceHandle` intact until DdDestroySurface? Both reference drivers assume so. Our `fb` counter and the audit measure it.
4. **Growable tables.** Should the table grow (per-local arrays, the DDK's advice) instead of having a fixed 12288 limit? Revisit only if a measured peak passes about 6000.
5. **At the limit.** Keep answering `DD_OK` (today's tolerance, now loud and counted), or fail CreateSurfaceEx with `DDERR_OUTOFMEMORY` as the DDK's general rule says? Failing propagates `E_OUTOFMEMORY` to the game (`DDKH` §4.5.7).
6. **TEXBLT with a larger source.** If `updlod` comes back "unmatched" with `sub == 0`, our TEXBLT copies nothing when the source chain is larger than the destination. 3dfx matched levels by size (`VH5/D6DP2.C:3892-3911`). That is a new TEXBLT gap for the plan, not G42.
7. **Plan-level numbering.** The two `D3DFeat` bits and the 513 what codes (24-29 already contended) are for the plan owner to allocate.
8. **3dfx's W2K attach-list anomaly** (`VH5/D7D3D.C:517-556`, PRS 12121). An attach list can name a Z buffer that was destroyed and not yet re-created. Our walk reads every attached surface's `lpSurfMore`, today and after this change. Not seen on XP. The audit would not detect a read of freed memory, so only a crash would show it.
