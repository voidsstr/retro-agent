# Open-weight LLM leaderboard standings, late November 2026, for a 128 GB-VRAM shortlist

Research date: 2026-11-24. Every download size below was MEASURED that day with the Hugging Face API
(`https://huggingface.co/api/models/<repo>?blobs=true`, summing `siblings[].size` of only the files one
format/quant needs; GB = 10^9 bytes). "Fits" means weights at most about 110 GB, which leaves KV-cache room in 128 GB.
Two measurement traps: (1) several official repos ship the same weights twice or three times (Mistral ships
`consolidated.safetensors` plus HF shards; gpt-oss-120b ships root, `original/` and `metal/` copies), so I give
the single-format size; (2) HF's "params" metadata miscounts FP4/INT4 weights packed into U8/I8 tensors (e.g. it
reports MiniMax-M3.1-Flash as 220.5B where the vendor says 428B). Where HF and the vendor disagree I give both.

## Q1. What are the top open-weight models overall right now, and the top ones in each size class?

### Takeaway
On Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5 (launched late October 2026; much harsher scale than v4), the open-weight
leaders are giants that cannot fit in 128 GB: Kimi K3.1 Pro (46-47), Tencent Hy4 (35), then DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 and
GLM-5.3 (32). In the band that can fit, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next and GLM-5.3-Flash lead at 28. MiMo-V2.6-Flash (27) just misses
at 2-bit. Ling-3.2-air is next (23, vendor-cited). Then come the dense ~27-33B models at about 20: Qwen3.8-27B, Inkling 2 Mini
and Nanbeige4.3-33B. On OpenRouter, usage is dominated by the cheap mid-size open MoEs: DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, Hy4 flash and
GLM-5.3-Flash.

### Inferences
- The v5 scale crushes older and smaller models: gpt-oss-120b and Gemma 4 31B score 4, against 20 for Qwen3.8-27B. That makes v5 a coarse discriminator among the models that fit, so task benchmarks (Terminal-Bench, SWE-bench Pro, DeepSWE) should break ties. Even those are mostly vendor-reported for this class.
- Harness matters more than model on Terminal-Bench. GLM-5.3-Flash scores 84.3% on AA's TB 2.1 but 37.0% on tbench 4.1. Models run with mini-swe-agent at reasoning "none" (Hy4 Flash, MiMo V2.6 Flash, Nemotron 3.5 Super VL) land near the bottom of tbench 4.1. Their low numbers there should not be read as model quality.
- None of the reference downloads (GLM-5.1, GLM-4.6, gpt-oss-120b, Qwen3-235B-2507, DeepSeek-V3.2-Exp, DeepSeek-V4-Pro, R1-0528, Kimi-K2-0905, and others) is near the current open frontier. GLM-4.6, Qwen3-235B-2507 and DeepSeek-V3.2 sit at Arena text #131-#136, and DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 scores 23 on AA v5.
- The candidates that fit and rank highest on independent evidence are Qwen3.8-Flash-Next and GLM-5.3-Flash (AA v5 28, top-3 open on AA's TB 2.1, high OpenRouter usage for GLM-5.3-Flash). Ling-3.2-air comes next, though its 23 is vendor-cited and unconfirmed.

### Inferences
- Suggested evidence-ordered shortlist for 128 GB:
  1. Qwen3.8-Flash-Next at UD-IQ4_XS/Q3_K_XL. AA v5 28 and the top open model on AA's TB 2.1. Its 51B N-gram embedding is "more amenable to offloading" than experts, so a higher-precision build with the embeddings in system RAM is plausible.
  2. GLM-5.3-Flash at UD-Q2_K_XL. AA v5 28, MIT, very high real-world usage. The cost is 2-bit and only about 19 GB left for KV cache.
  3. DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 at UD-IQ3_XXS. MIT; no v5 score found.
  4. Ling-3.2-air at Q5_K_M/Q6_K. MIT, a day old, vendor numbers only.
  5. Qwen3.8-27B at BF16, the safest high-precision pick. Apache-2.0.
  6. Inkling 2 Mini at native NVFP4/MXFP8, 20 GB. Apache-2.0; claims parity with Qwen3.8-27B at about a third of the size.
  7. Nemotron 3.5 Super VL at official NVFP4, 75 GB. Permissive licence; weaker AA score.
  8. Nanbeige4.3-33B at BF16.
- Mistral Medium 3.5 128B is dense, so it decodes all 128B parameters per token. With AA v5 at 5, it is hard to justify against the MoEs above.

### Gaps
- Exact licence texts for qwen-community-1.0, MiniMax Community, Tencent Hy v1.0, StepFun 1.0 and Mistral Medium 3.5's modified MIT were not read first-hand. Only secondary summaries were available.
- No measured AA score exists for DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 on v5. Ling-3.2-air's 23 is vendor-cited only.
- The `nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Bolt-74B-A9B-FP8` and `-NVFP4` repo names returned HTTP 401 (gated or nonexistent), so no official 8-bit or 4-bit Bolt size was measured. The "24GB" build reported by [Magica](https://magica.com/news/nvidia-nemotron-3-5-bolt-weights-local-ai-memory) was not located.
- Active-parameter count for Mistral Small 4 was not verified.

## Q3. Which models released between August and November 2026 matter for this size class?

### Takeaway
The August-November wave added mid-size MoEs (roughly 120-320B total, 5-18B active) and strong ~26-33B models. The ones that matter for 128 GB are Qwen3.8-Flash-Next and Qwen3.8-27B (Aug), GLM-5.3-Flash (Aug), MiMo-V2.6-Flash (Sep), Nemotron 3.5 Super VL and Bolt (Oct), Nanbeige4.3-33B (Oct), and Inkling 2 Mini, Hy4-flash, Ling-3.2-air and ERNIE 5.5 Flash Preview (all Nov 17-23). DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 (Jul 31) sits just before the window and still matters. No new open LLM was found from OpenAI, Google (Gemma), Ai2 (OLMo) or ByteDance Seed in the window.

## Q4. How much quality is lost by the quantization needed to fit?

### Takeaway
Published evidence says 4-bit is close to lossless: NVFP4 checkpoints recover about 98-99% on average, and unsloth calls dynamic Q4/Q5 "mostly lossless". 2-bit keeps roughly 80% top-1 token agreement and is the lowest bit-width unsloth recommends for real use. 1-bit keeps about 72-79% top-1 agreement but loses 8-23% on agentic coding and breaks tool calling. So GLM-5.3-Flash at 2-bit will carry a real, unquantified penalty. Qwen3.8-Flash-Next at 3-4-bit should be close to its published scores. Nearly all of this data is self-published by quantizers or vendors.

### Cited Findings
- Unsloth on GLM-5.2 (744B/40B): Dynamic 1-bit (UD-IQ1_S) keeps about 76.2% top-1 token agreement while being about 86% smaller. Dynamic 2-bit (UD-IQ2_M, 239 GB) keeps about 82% at about 84% smaller. Dynamic 4-bit and 5-bit are described as "mostly lossless". Accuracy "drops more from 4-bit onward" on out-of-distribution tasks; KLD values appear only as charts — [Unsloth GLM-5.2 guide](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/glm-5.2).
- Unsloth Dynamic V3.0/3.1 page (Nov 2026):
  - Qwen3.8-27B: UD-IQ1_S (6.2 GB) keeps about 72% top-1. Divergence-300@32 falls from about 25% at UD-IQ2_S to under 8-10% at UD-Q2_K_XL (9.83 GB).
  - Kimi-K3 at 1-bit (62% smaller) keeps about 78.9% top-1.
  - Dynamic V3.1 at 1-bit: Qwen3.8-Flash-Next scores 54.0% DeepSWE v1.1 and 68.3% TB 2.1. DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017 at IQ1_S scores 65.8% DeepSWE, 69.5% TB 2.1 and 51.5% Automation Bench.
  - Recommendations: UD-Q2_K_XL is the lowest bit-width for real use. 1-bit quants loop, return empty responses without thinking, and "should not be used for tool calling or agentic work" — [Unsloth Dynamic 3.0](https://unsloth.ai/docs/basics/dynamic-3.0-ggufs).
- Full-precision reference points for those 1-bit runs. Qwen3.8-Flash-Next: DeepSWE 58.7 (vendor) — [Qwen card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next) — and TB 2.1 86.1% (AA) — [benchleader](https://www.benchleader.com/benchmarks/aa_terminalbench_21). DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017: DeepSWE 77.0 (Baidu's table) — [ERNIE card](https://huggingface.co/baidu/ERNIE-5.5-Flash-Preview) — or 74.2, and TB 2.1 90.6 — [Morph](https://www.morphllm.com/best-open-source-llm).
- Older but numeric (Unsloth Dynamic v2.0, Gemma 3 27B), as mean KLD vs BF16 / size:
  - IQ1_S 0.973 / 6.06 GB; IQ2_XXS 0.521 / 7.31 GB; Q2_K_XL 0.221 / 9.95 GB; Q3_K_XL 0.081 / 12.76 GB; Q4_K_XL 0.024 / 15.64 GB.
  - MMLU 5-shot: Q2_K_XL 68.70, Q3_K_XL 70.87, Q4_K_XL 71.47, Q8_0 71.60, against BF16 71.5 — [Unsloth Dynamic 3.0 page](https://unsloth.ai/docs/basics/dynamic-3.0-ggufs).
- Unsloth benchmarked Qwen3.6-35B-A3B GGUFs from several providers on KLD. Nearly all Unsloth quants sit on the KLD-vs-disk Pareto frontier, and MXFP4_MOE is an outlier for all — [Daniel Han on X](https://x.com/danielhanchen/status/2045169369723064449). Unsloth's Dynamic 3-bit DeepSeek V3.1 scored 75.6% on Aider polyglot (Sept 2025) — [Unsloth Aider page](https://unsloth.ai/docs/basics/unsloth-dynamic-2.0-ggufs/unsloth-dynamic-ggufs-on-aider-polyglot).
- NVFP4 4-bit on a 501B MoE (RedHatAI's Beam 1.0 card, BF16 → NVFP4): SWE-V 81.6 → 80.7, SWE-Pro v1 66.1 → 65.6, TB 2.1 80.1 → 78.4, GPQA 90.6 → 89.3, HLE 36.0 → 34.8, SciCode 49.2 → 46.9. That is about 99.0% mean recovery across 22 benchmarks (my arithmetic from the card's table) — [RedHatAI Beam NVFP4](https://huggingface.co/RedHatAI/Beam-1.0-501B-A23B-NVFP4).
- NVFP4 on Nemotron 3.5 Super VL (BF16 → NVFP4): MMLU-ProX 76.91 → 73.76, DeepSWE 24.65 → 18.2, WebVoyager 77.72 → 66.31, TB 2.1 60.67 → 62.78. That averages about 98.3% across 38 rows (my arithmetic) but includes large agentic drops — [NVIDIA card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Super-VL-120B-A12B-NVFP4).

### Inferences
- For a 128 GB budget, prefer a model that fits at 3-5 bits (Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, Ling-3.2-air, DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 at IQ3) over a larger model squeezed to 2-bit (GLM-5.3-Flash). The 2-bit penalty, about 18% top-1 disagreement on GLM-5.2, is larger than the AA v5 gap between those models, which tie at 28.
- 1-bit builds of giants (Beam 1.0 at 103 GB; DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, smallest 233 GB, does not fit anyway) are unsuitable for agentic or tool use on Unsloth's own advice.

### Gaps
- No independent, non-vendor KLD or benchmark comparison of 2-3-bit GGUFs for GLM-5.3-Flash, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next or DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 was found. Neither was any r/LocalLLaMA measurement; Reddit was not reached.
- The top-1 agreement figures are not task accuracy, and the KLD figures for 2026 MoEs exist only as charts. The 1-bit retention ratios above mix harnesses across sources, so they are indicative only.
